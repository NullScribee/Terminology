📖 What is Medical Terminology?

Medical terminology is the language used by health care practitioners — the precise way of describing the human body, its components, processes, conditions and procedures.

History

  • Originated as early as the 1st century B.C., when Hippocrates practised medicine.
  • With advancements in medicine, new terms have evolved to reflect these innovations.

Why it is easy once you see the pattern

Becoming familiar with medical terminology is like learning a new language. Basic rules for building terms help you build and translate thousands of words. You must be able to put words together — or break them apart — like the pieces of a puzzle.

🧩 The Word Elements

ElementDefinitionExample
Word root ⭐⭐ The foundation / core of the term that contains its primary (fundamental) meaning. It frequently names a body part. cardi (heart) → carditis
Prefix ⭐⭐ Element attached to the beginning of a word/root that adjusts or qualifies its meaning. tachy (fast) + cardia
Suffix ⭐⭐⭐ Element placed at the end of a root/combining form that changes the meaning of the word. cardi + itis (inflammation)
Combining vowel ⭐⭐ A vowel (usually o, sometimes i) added to the end of a root to make the word easier to pronounce. It has no meaning of its own. gastrotomy · gastroenteritis
Combining form ⭐⭐ Word root + combining vowel, written root/o. Used to build compound terms. cardi/o, gastr/o, hepat/o, end/o

Facts about word roots

  • All medical terms have at least one word root.
  • Most word roots are derived from Greek or Latin.
  • Two different roots may have the same meaning (dermat/o & cutane/o = skin; nephr/o & ren/o = kidney; stomat/o & or/o = mouth).
  • Greek roots → used to build words describing disease, condition, treatment or diagnosis.
  • Latin roots → used to describe anatomical structures.

Facts about prefixes

  • Attached to the beginning of a word or root.
  • Do all medical terms have a prefix? NO.
  • Adding/changing a prefix changes the meaning of the word.
  • It indicates: a number, time, position, direction, colour, speed, size or negation.
  • The same prefixes are also used in ordinary English (e.g. antibiotic, extraordinary).

Facts about suffixes

  • Placed at the end of a word root or combining form and changes the meaning of the word.
  • A suffix describes: a pathology (disease/abnormality), a symptom, a surgical or diagnostic procedure, or a part of speech.
  • It never gives the primary anatomical meaning — that is the root's job.

The 3 rules for building medical words guaranteed question

RuleWhat happensExample
Rule #1A root links to a suffix that begins with a vowel → NO combining vowel is used.hepat + itis = hepatitis
gastr + itis = gastritis
Rule #2A root links to a suffix that begins with a consonant → the combining vowel IS used.arthro + scope = arthroscope
hepato + cyte = hepatocyte
Rule #3To link one root to another root → the combining vowel IS used (even if the second root starts with a vowel).osteo + chondr + itis = osteochondritis
gastro + enter + itis = gastroenteritis
Never write two vowels together: we say gastritis — NOT gastroitis. If the suffix already starts with a vowel, the combining vowel is dropped.
Special spelling rules: (1) when the suffix begins with rh, the r is doubled → rhinorrhea, -rrhaphy. (2) when a word ends in x, the x changes to g or c → pharyngeal, thoracotomy.

How to define any medical word — in 3 steps

  1. Define the first part of the word (the prefix, or the first root) → gastr/o = stomach
  2. Define the middle part(s) (combining forms) → enter = intestine
  3. Define the suffix (the ending) → -itis = inflammation
gastroenteritis = gastr/o (stomach) + enter (intestine) + -itis (inflammation) = inflammation of the stomach and intestine

How the suffix changes the meaning — the gastr- family

gastr- + -megaly= enlargement of the stomach

gastr- + -oma= tumour of the stomach

🧬 Body Structure & Levels of Organization

The body is made up of several levels of structure and function. Each level builds on the previous one and contributes to the structure and function of the entire organism.
cell→ tissue→ organ→ system→ organism
from least to most complex

1 · Cell

  • The study of the body at the cellular level is called cytology.
  • The cell is the structural and functional unit of life.
  • Body cells perform all activities associated with life: utilizing food, eliminating waste and reproducing.
  • Cells consist of a cell membrane that encloses the cytoplasm and a nucleus.

2 · Tissue

  • Groups of cells that perform a specialized activity.
  • The study of tissues is called histology.
  • Between the cells are nonliving intercellular substances that provide pathways for cellular interaction.
  • More than 200 cell types compose the four major tissues: muscle, nervous, epithelial and connective.

3 · Organ

  • Body structures that perform specialized functions.
  • Composed of at least two or more tissue types.
  • Example: the stomach is made of connective, muscle, epithelial and nervous tissue.

4 · System

  • Composed of varying numbers of organs and other structures that have similar or related functions.
  • GI system organs: esophagus, stomach, small intestine, bowel. Accessory structures: liver, gallbladder, pancreas.
  • Purpose: digest food, remove and use its nutrients, and expel waste products.
  • Other systems: reproductive, respiratory, urinary, cardiovascular…

⚠️ Do Not Confuse — the traps that cost marks

cyst/o vs cholecyst/o

cystitis = inflammation of the urinary bladder.
cholecystitis = inflammation of the gall bladder.
cystoscopy looks in the bladder; cholecystectomy removes the gall bladder.

-oma vs -megaly vs -itis

hepatoma = liver tumour.
hepatomegaly = enlarged liver.
hepatitis = inflamed liver.

-ostomy vs -otomy vs -ectomy

colostomy = permanent opening.
colotomy = incision into the colon.
colectomy = removal of the colon.

-graphy vs -graph vs -gram

mammography = the procedure.
mammograph = the machine.
mammogram = the X-ray image.

-desis vs -pexy

arthrodesis = joint fusion/binding.
orchiopexy = surgical fixation/suspension of the testis.

-ectasis vs -megaly

bronchiectasis = dilation of the bronchi.
cardiomegaly = enlargement of the heart.

hypo- vs hyper-

hypoglycemia = low sugar · hypotension = low BP · hypothyroidism = underactive.
hyper = above normal / excessive.

tachy- vs brady- & -cardia vs -pnea

Heart: tachycardia / bradycardia. Breathing: tachypnea / bradypnea. Never mix the organ!

ab- vs ad-

abduction = away from the midline.
adduction = toward the midline.

prone vs supine

prone = face down. supine = face up (the position for examining the abdomen).

ipsilateral vs contralateral

ipsilateral = same side. contralateral = opposite side (right arm + left leg).

meningitis vs encephalitis

meningitis = inflammation of the membranes (meninges).
encephalitis = inflammation of the brain itself.

myel/o has two meanings

myelitis = inflammation of the spinal cord (or bone marrow).
myeloma = tumour of bone marrow.

gloss/o vs gingiv/o vs stomat/o

glossitis = tongue · gingivitis = gums · stomatitis = mouth.

epistaxis vs hemoptysis vs hematemesis

epistaxis = nosebleed · hemoptysis = coughing blood · hematemesis = vomiting blood.

sign vs symptom

Symptom = what the patient complains of (headache, pain). Sign = what is measured/observed (temp 39 °C, BP 140/90).

  15. Levels: cell → tissue → organ → system → organism. Cytology = cells, histology = tissues.

  17. abduction away, adduction toward · prone face down, supine face up · ipsilateral same side, contralateral opposite.
  18. Rules: suffix starts with a vowel → no combining vowel (hepatitis). Suffix starts with a consonant → use o (arthroscope). Two roots → always use the combining vowel (gastroenteritis).
Exam technique: (1) find the root first — it tells you the organ; (2) read the suffix — it tells you what is happening to that organ; (3) only then look at the prefix — it tells you where/when/how much. Two organs in the word? Then both are involved (gastro-enter-itis, hepato-splenomegaly). Any option that names the wrong organ is instantly wrong, no matter how long it is.
